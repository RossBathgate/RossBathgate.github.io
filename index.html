Zero-Knowledge Proofs: Removing Interaction from Sigma Protocols in the Non-programmable Random Oracle Model with an Online Extractor
A zero-knowledge proof is a method of proving a statement without revealing anything other than the fact the statement is true. This seemingly paradoxical construction has become increasingly popular and widely applicable, with applications to electronic voting and auctions, cryptographic key distribution, digital banking, and many others. This project aims to build on the work of Kondi and Shelat, taking inspiration from Ciampi, to achieve a non-interactive protocol (proof of knowledge) with preferable security properties and assumptions that better match the real-world than many alternative protocols. A new transform is constructed, and an implementation is provided (in Python) to compare runtime against existing approaches. Under certain assumptions, the construction runs efficiently in polynomial time and empirical results indicate that the new construction outperforms an alternative approach of Fischlin upon which much of the project is based.

Gaussian Processes for Machine Learning
This report examines Gaussian Processes (GPs) in machine learning, focusing on regression, classification, training point selection and optimisation. We introduce three applications: a tailored kernel design to model domain-specific patterns, GP regression for analysing noisy snowline data, and probabilistic classification of heart disease using approximation methods to quantify uncertainty. We also compare strategies for selecting training points to improve efficiency and propose a Bayesian optimisation framework that updates hyperparameters dynamically. Through theoretical analysis and case studies, this work demonstrates how GPs balance interpretability and performance in real-world scenarios.
